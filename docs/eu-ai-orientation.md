# EU AI Act — High-Level Orientation

**Not legal advice. Not a compliance assessment. Orientation only.**

## Purpose

Give AIC contributors a shared, high-level vocabulary when discussing how concepts in the EU AI Act *might* relate to categories of system.  
This is a sketch for internal orientation, not an applicability analysis for any specific product or deployment.

## Key ideas (simplified)

The EU AI Act uses a risk-based approach. At a very high level:

- Some practices are prohibited.
- Certain systems are treated as high-risk and face stricter requirements.
- Transparency and other obligations apply to additional categories.
- General-purpose / systemic-risk discussions exist for certain models and services.

Exact definitions, annexes, and obligations evolve and must be read from official sources and competent advice.

## Orientation questions for AIC-style components

When reflecting on a component, useful questions include:

1. Is the component an “AI system” under the relevant definition, or is it primarily coordination / policy / formal / tooling infrastructure?
2. Does it perform safety-critical or rights-impacting automated decisions without meaningful human oversight?
3. Does the design retain an explicit, non-bypassable human gate for consequential outcomes?
4. Is the intended use research, TestNet experimentation, or a real-world high-impact deployment?
5. What transparency and documentation artifacts already exist (or are planned) that would support accountability?

## AIC design intents that are relevant to the conversation

- Ethical Kernel with Allow / Deny / NeedHuman.
- Fail-closed default.
- Explicit pre-Covenant / under-claim public posture.
- Preference against token rule-power and against capture by pure commercial or pure state logics (Third Path).

These intents may support arguments for careful, human-centric design; they do **not** constitute an exemption or a formal classification under the Act.

## What to do with this document

- Use it to structure internal discussion.
- Do **not** cite it as evidence of compliance or non-applicability.
- For any real deployment or market-facing claim in scope of the Act, obtain jurisdiction-appropriate professional advice and follow official guidance.

## Sources to prefer

Always prefer primary legal texts, official guidance from EU institutions, and qualified counsel over secondary summaries (including this one).
