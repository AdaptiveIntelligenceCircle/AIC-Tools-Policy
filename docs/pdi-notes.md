# Public Digital Infrastructure (PDI) — Orientation Notes

**Not legal advice. Orientation only.**

## What “PDI orientation” means here

Public Digital Infrastructure, in AIC conversation, refers to digital layers that:

- serve broad coordination or meaning-preserving functions,
- are designed to resist capture by a single commercial or single state logic,
- remain inspectable and accountable,
- treat human judgment and fail-closed constraints as first-class.

This is a **design and governance orientation**, not a legal status and not a claim that any current AIC component *is* recognized public infrastructure.

## Why it appears in policy tools

When classifying risk or writing disclosure notes, it is useful to ask:

- Are we building something that could later be treated as shared infrastructure?
- If so, do our transparency, succession, and human-gate practices match that ambition?
- Are we accidentally creating private rule-power (including token-based) over a layer that should stay neutral?

## Practical questions

1. Is the component’s rule-set influenceable by payment or token weight? (AIC answer intended: no.)
2. Are decision paths and failure modes documented and inspectable?
3. Is there a clear path for human override / NeedHuman?
4. Is succession / continuity addressed without concentrating unaccountable power?
5. Does public communication stay under-claim so that users and authorities are not misled about maturity?

## Relationship to Third Path

Third Path (neither pure state capture nor pure commercial rule-power) and PDI orientation reinforce each other.  
Tools in this repository should never be used to argue for immunity; they should be used to keep design conversations honest.
